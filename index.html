How this works

Every Monday a GitHub Action downloads all planets of 1.6–4 R⊕ from the NASA Exoplanet Archive and searches arXiv (astro-ph.EP) for new JWST papers that name those planets. Hand-reviewed results (instruments, composition, source) come from data/jwst_results.json. A planet flagged New paper was found automatically and has not been reviewed yet; its composition is not summarized until someone adds it to that file.

Publication status: each cited paper is checked for a journal version. “Published” means NASA ADS lists publisher full text or a refereed record, or the arXiv entry or Crossref shows a journal article and DOI (the badge links to it when a DOI is known). “Accepted” comes from the authors' arXiv comment. “No journal version found” means none of those sources shows one yet, so treat the paper as a preprint. Preprints are checked again every week.

Classification: for the reviewed planets this is the label used in the cited paper's title or abstract (see Source). For all other planets no per-planet label exists in the catalog, so the page shows a size class from the radius alone: <1.25 R⊕ Earth-size, 1.25–1.6 super-Earth (likely rocky), 1.6–4 sub-Neptune, 4–6 Neptune-size. These are conventional cut-offs, not a measurement of composition, and a planet's literature label can differ.

“No published result in tracker” only means this tracker has no reviewed paper for that planet. Some curated masses and temperatures are rounded; verify against the cited source before relying on a row.
